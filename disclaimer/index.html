Three things worth being clear about before you buy.

Last updated: 12 November 2026

Color will not match exactly

What you see on your screen is not what will come off a printer, and what comes off one printer is not what will come off another. Screens emit light and printers lay down ink; they are different things, and they do not agree.

On top of that, the material changes the result. The same file printed on cotton, on coated paper, on uncoated paper, on vinyl, and on a ceramic surface will produce five different results, and none of them will be the color on your monitor.

So: test print before a large production run. Order a sample, look at it under the light the finished thing will live in, and adjust from there. This is ordinary practice for anyone producing printed goods, and skipping it is how expensive mistakes happen.

We make no claim about color accuracy, and we do not supply or guarantee any color profile.

Product types are named as examples, not as promises

Across this site you will see product types named — fabric, apparel, wallpaper, packaging, stationery, print-on-demand items, websites, digital designs, and others. They are named to describe the kinds of things the patterns can be used for, so you can tell whether a product suits your work.

Naming a product type is not a statement that a pattern has been tested on it, is optimized for it, will reproduce well on it, or meets any standard or requirement that applies to it. Whether a pattern suits a particular process, material, or printer is for you to establish, which is another reason to test print.
